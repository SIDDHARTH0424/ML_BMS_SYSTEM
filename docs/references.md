# References — Driving EMS Extension

All three checked via live web search (August 2026) against publisher
pages before use — see `docs/research_grounding.md` for verification notes.

1. **Ananganó-Alvarado, G., Umaña-Morel, I., & Keith-Norambuena, B. (2025).**
   Reinforcement learning in electric vehicle energy management: a
   comprehensive open-access review of methods, challenges, and future
   innovations. *Frontiers in Future Transportation*, 6, 1555250.
   DOI: [10.3389/ffutr.2025.1555250](https://doi.org/10.3389/ffutr.2025.1555250)
   Reason: motivates including dynamic vehicle states (acceleration,
   regenerative braking, road grade, load variation) in the new EMS design.

2. **He, H., Meng, X., Wang, Y., Khajepour, A., An, X., Wang, R., & Sun, F.
   (2024).** Deep reinforcement learning based energy management
   strategies for electrified vehicles: Recent advances and perspectives.
   *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews*, 192, 114248.
   DOI: [10.1016/j.rser.2023.114248](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2023.114248)
   Reason: guides state/action/reward design methodology and PPO/DRL
   algorithm selection for the driving EMS.

3. **"Reinforcement learning as a control layer for electric vehicle
   interaction with multi-energy systems: A comprehensive review" (2026).**
   *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews* (ScienceDirect).
   DOI/URL: [10.1016/j.rser.2026...](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364032126000328)
   Reason: motivates the hierarchical safety-layer-as-hard-constraint
   architecture (§15 of the master task) and grounds the "known
   limitations" section in real, current literature on RL-EV deployment
   risk rather than an assumed list.
